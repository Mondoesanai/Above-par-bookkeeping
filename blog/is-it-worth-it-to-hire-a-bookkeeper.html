Is It Worth It to Hire a Bookkeeper?

2026-09-24

Short answer: for most growing businesses, yes — but it depends on how much time your books are taking you and how confident you feel in your numbers. Here's how to think it through.

What a bookkeeper actually does

A bookkeeper keeps your financial records accurate and current. That includes reconciling accounts, tracking income and expenses, managing invoices, and processing payroll. The goal is simple: clean books and clear numbers, so you always know where your business stands.

When your records are organized, tax time is smoother, cash flow is easier to plan, and you can make decisions based on real numbers instead of guesses.

Signs it might be worth hiring one

  • You're spending hours each month untangling receipts, invoices, or bank statements instead of running your business.
  • You're not sure if your books are accurate, or you've fallen behind and need a cleanup.
  • Payroll, budgeting, or forecasting feel more complicated than they should.
  • You want to focus on growth instead of admin work.

If any of these sound familiar, outsourcing your bookkeeping can free up real time and reduce stress — even if you're comfortable with numbers.

When doing it yourself might still work

If your business is very simple, your transaction volume is low, and you genuinely enjoy staying on top of the details, DIY bookkeeping can work fine for a while. The key is consistency — books that get updated sporadically tend to create bigger problems down the road, like inaccurate financials or scrambling at tax time.

Many business owners start out doing their own books and then bring in help once things get busier or more complex, like when they add employees and need payroll, or start juggling more invoices and vendors.

What to look for in outsourced help

Whether you're hiring locally or working remotely, look for someone who understands your day-to-day needs and communicates clearly.
